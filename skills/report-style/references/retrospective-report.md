# Retrospective or incident report

## Use when

The execution itself is the subject: the reader needs to know what happened, why the
outcome occurred, what the evidence supports, and what should change next.

## Aspects

| Aspect | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Intent and outcome | What was expected, and what actually happened? |
| Impact | Who or what was affected, for how long, and with what severity? |
| Causal sequence | Which few events are necessary to explain the outcome? |
| Detection and response | How was the condition observed, contained, recovered, or made safe? |
| Contributing conditions | Which technical, process, or environmental facts made the outcome possible? |
| Root cause | What explanation accounts for the evidence without collapsing into blame? |
| What held up | Which practices or controls produced useful evidence or limited damage? |
| What failed | Which assumptions, controls, or working methods did not hold? |
| Changes | What will be kept, stopped, or changed, with an owner or destination when actionable? |
| Claim boundary | Which causal links are demonstrated, plausible, contested, or unknown? |

For a process retrospective, impact may mean wasted work, delayed learning, or an invalid
claim. For an operational incident, include recovery state and remaining exposure.

## Chronology

Chronology belongs here when order establishes causality, detection delay, or recovery.
Keep only causal events. A full command transcript still belongs in evidence, not in the
narrative.

## Common failures

- a diary with no causal model;
- a root cause that is only the last visible error;
- lessons with no evidence-bearing incident;
- action items that do not alter the condition that produced the outcome.
