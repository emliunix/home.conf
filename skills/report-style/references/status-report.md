# Status report

## Use when

The reader needs the present delivery picture: what is usable, what remains open, what is
blocked, and where their decision is needed.

## Aspects

| Aspect | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| As-of anchor | At what time, revision, environment, or release is this status true? |
| Topic map | What are the few active topics, stated in domain or product language? |
| Available now | What is actually usable or landed, with the evidence threshold met? |
| Open work | What behavior or contract is not complete yet? |
| Blockers | What blocks each topic, what is the impact, and is it technical, external, or a decision? |
| Decision requests | What exactly must the reader decide, and what changes under each choice? |
| Accepted exposure | Which known limitation is explicitly tolerated and therefore is not a blocker? |
| Next action | What is the next consequential action after the blocker clears? |
| Claim boundary | What was not rechecked, is changing concurrently, or is only reported by another seat? |

Explain blockers together with their topic and consequence. A table of labels such as
"draft", "reviewed", or "80%" is not a status report unless the reader can tell what the
work means and why it is open.

## Chronology

Include dates, milestones, or dependency order only when they explain the present state.
Do not list every completed command or implementation step. Completed work is normally
compressed into the capability or evidence it produced.

## Common failures

- reporting percentages with no acceptance basis;
- listing blockers without the decision or external change that would clear them;
- treating accepted risk as unresolved work;
- mixing another contributor's unverified message with directly observed state.
