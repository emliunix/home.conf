# Research report

## Use when

The reader needs a source-grounded answer, landscape, comparison, or recommendation about
an external technology, practice, or product.

## Aspects

| Aspect | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Research question | What decision or understanding is the research meant to support? |
| Scope and exclusions | Which products, versions, jurisdictions, or use cases are inside and outside the corpus? |
| Source corpus | Which primary sources were captured, when, and with what gaps? |
| Findings | What do the sources directly establish? |
| Mechanism | How does the relevant behavior actually work? |
| Comparison | Which decision-relevant dimensions distinguish the nearest options? |
| Analysis | What is inferred by combining sources, and how confident is that inference? |
| Recommendation | What choice follows for the stated constraints, rather than in the abstract? |
| Conflicts and unknowns | Where do sources disagree, remain silent, or appear stale? |
| Claim boundary | What was measured, documented, inferred, or not verified? |

Keep source digest separate from analysis. Prefer primary sources and make a comparison
dimension earn its place by connecting it to the reader's decision.

## Chronology

The order of searches and note-taking is not relevant. Publication dates, version history,
and changed vendor behavior are relevant when they affect whether a claim is current.

## Common failures

- treating a vendor claim as measured behavior;
- comparing feature counts without the reader's constraints;
- hiding unavailable primary sources behind confident synthesis;
- making a universal recommendation from a project-specific tradeoff.
